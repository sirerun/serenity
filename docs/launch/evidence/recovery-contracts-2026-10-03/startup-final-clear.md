The four previous blockers are resolved in the contract:

1. **Head, active generation, activation, and first-append race:** `JournalPosition` separately represents a positive active generation, its last object, predecessor seal, and genesis or successor-allocation identity ([lines 73–79](<[external evidence path]>)). It defines an authenticated empty successor as an empty active generation anchored by the exact preceding seal and allocation ID ([lines 165–171](<[external evidence path]>)). A durable namespace reservation and provider-enforced write-deny fence precede observation; the reservation binds the exact observed history, writer-use set, position, inventory, and policy epoch; activation CASes that reservation while the deny fence is held. The lease carries the exact first-append predecessor, and `NewJournalAt` must fail closed if the next key/head differs. The fence and active-writer epoch remain held through service replay and publication ([lines 156–171](<[external evidence path]>), [service seam lines 195–197](<[external evidence path]>)). These requirements close the design race rather than delegating its resolution to an implementation worker.

2. **Read-only and writable capabilities:** `JournalObjectReader` exposes only `Get` and `ListAfter`; only `JournalObjectStore` adds `PutIfAbsent`. Staged service admission receives `DeletionJournalReader`; only a successfully activated `WriterLease` exposes the writable store ([lines 57–66, 88–90, 120–139](<[external evidence path]>), [lines 144–145](<[external evidence path]>)). It also requires type-level controls that prove staged code has no append, seal, or conditional-write method ([line 224](<[external evidence path]>)).

3. **Authenticated restart identity:** the trusted constructor binds `RuntimeIdentitySource`; `Restart` obtains its signed invocation from that source before journal I/O or authority preparation, and the authority validates it. Callers cannot inject an environment/config identity or alternate invocation. The corresponding types and constructor are explicit ([lines 26–44](<[external evidence path]>)).

4. **Namespace-wide credential and principal fence:** the contract requires an exhaustive authority-observed inventory, with explicit route metadata and no caller completeness flag. It covers workload and assumed-role sessions, renewal/metadata/environment routes, other application and operator/admin/break-glass principals, automation/replication, policy and ACL controls, lifecycle/deletion, and principals able to alter the fence or inventory. It requires denial/revocation and provider readback, and refuses to return a read-capable reservation if completeness or enforcement cannot be proven ([lines 96–113, 156–163](<[external evidence path]>)).

`JournalWriterUse{Generation, WriterID}` is adequate under the now-explicit global uniqueness rule for writer IDs. The semantics require unique first-object-use order across every verified entry **and seal**, collapse repeated uses of one issuance, bind every writer to one durable issuance for its deployment/namespace/generation/principal, and reject missing, incomplete, conflicting, or unknown provenance ([lines 68–71, 173](<[external evidence path]>)). The owner assignment is concrete: contracts own the read-only observation and types; deletion owns extracting entry and seal uses plus the history digest; service consumes the exact proof/lease tuple; startup owns identity, reservation, authority, inventory, and lease. It specifies mutually exclusive recovery vs authenticated-startup dependency sets and keeps recovery's existing sealed checks unchanged ([lines 174–197, 229–237](<[external evidence path]>)).
