# Independent contract review: recovery pin owner proposal

Verdict: HOLD for source freeze.

Reviewed exact commit `c7d0a3d628124e1c6ed33a38d1d86e19b0ed88c3` in a fresh detached SSD clone. Reviewed `docs/plans/recovery-pin-owner-source-proposed-contract-2026-10-03.md`, SHA-256 `d2f04ccc03e3d32740b0f3eb228a8e23f1c6b8fd557ede1dd73c5a987e708f1a`. Compared its producer-facing contract against the landed/qualified backup producer source in the snapshot pin absence implementation checkout. This is a documentation-only review; no source or build actions were taken.

The proposed boundary is appropriately narrow. It does not claim READY, restore/effect, COMMITTED restore, admission, provider, or startup-factory authority. One durable owner is proposed, and the owner does not mint `PinCommittedRestoreComplete`. Its expected-identity bootstrap matches the amended producer constructor shape: preflight identity, owner bound to that opaque token, and required fourth `expected` argument to `NewSnapshotLeaseStore`. Cancellation consumes the exact callback-scoped proof with the retained identity; tombstones are permanent, and N+1 requires a fresh staged lease. The proposal also correctly accounts for the producer's actual `SnapshotPinRef` getters and the narrower `PinReleaseAuthorization` tuple by requiring linkage to complete owner history and its record version.

Three contract gaps prevent a reliable, independently implementable source freeze:

1. **Public API data types are not specified.** The API introduces `SnapshotPinOwnerOptions`, `PinOwnerReservation`, and `PinAbandonReason` without defining their fields or constructors. In particular, the caller needs a normative way to obtain the exact `planRef` and immutable `reservationVersion` from `PinOwnerReservation` to invoke the producer's `Pin` API, and the owner needs a defined way to validate and bind `operationID` retries. Define exported fields/getters and validation/zero-value behavior, plus the exact options (root, limits/support matrix, identity token) and constructor argument relationship. The lifecycle-interface signatures themselves match the current backup interface.

2. **Closed wire enums and transition encoding are not exhaustive.** The document requires closed `event`, `state`, `attempt_state`, `release_disposition`, and `reason_code` enums, but does not enumerate the complete `event` or `reason_code` values nor provide a normative event-to-state/field-presence transition table. It lists several states and one committed record example, but does not define exact record payload values for reservation, attempt begin/cancel, abandonment, and release transitions. Source authors would have to invent wire values and which fields must be empty/nonempty, risking incompatible recovery histories. Enumerate all v1 values and exact allowed transitions/required field matrices; keep future-state behavior explicit as read-only/unsupported.

3. **Promised canonical vectors are absent.** Section 132 names additional vectors for genesis reservation, pending pin, canceled N, fresh N+1, abandonment, release authorization, RELEASING, RELEASED, and future consumer state, but supplies no bytes or digests for those vectors. Only a superblock vector and one committed-pin record are concrete. Supply the advertised vectors (including predecessor chains and expected errors) or narrow the source-freeze criterion to one fully specified canonical transition set with independently reproducible vectors for every current authority-bearing event.

These are contract completeness issues, not objections to the bounded lifecycle or identity design. The narrow component can proceed to source freeze once the three items are made normative and the updated exact document is reviewed. This does not change the separate whole-store HOLD: no factory, full plan store, effects proof, READY path, or production acceptance is established here.
